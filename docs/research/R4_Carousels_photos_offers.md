# R4 Carousels, photos and offers: NUMA Instagram Playbook: Carousels, Single Photos and FTC-Safe Offer Posts (2025–2026)

> Deep-research result for prompt R4, received 2026-11-13. Orchestrator review notes: docs/research/REVIEW.md. WARNING: this report assumed Numa is a yoga/pilates class studio (mats, "Slow Flow", teachers) and ignored the sauna/plunge/breathwork context. Use its evidence, offer rules and metrics; treat its template copy as structure only, rewritten with Numa's real facts and brand words.

NUMA should build its organic Instagram around saveable, sendable educational and "what to expect" carousels (roughly 6–10 slides, one idea per slide, a strong cover and an equally strong slide 2), back them with people-first single photos of the real space and real teachers, and keep the $49 first-month offer to a small, planned share of feed posts with full terms in plain sight. The best-supported 2025–2026 evidence says carousels earn the most engagement per post, Reels earn the most reach, single images trail on both, and Instagram's ranking leans on likes, sends and watch time per person reached. The legal side is just as clear: testimonials can't carry claims NUMA couldn't make itself, paid creators need clear "#ad"-style disclosure, fake reviews and fake urgency are now enforcement targets, and a $49 intro month that rolls into a paid membership must spell out its renewal terms.

## TL;DR

- **Format:** Carousels are NUMA's engagement and save engine. Socialinsider's 2025 data puts carousels at 0.55% engagement vs 0.52% for Reels and 0.37% for images, and Buffer's 4M-post study found carousels drew 114% more engagement than single images while Reels drew 36% more reach than carousels. Use carousels to teach and reassure, Reels to reach new people, and single photos for human warmth and place.
- **Offers:** Promote the $49 first month in about 1 of every 5–6 feed posts. Wrap it in "what your first visit looks like" reassurance rather than hype. Use only real deadlines and a real regular price, and state renewal terms up front. Measure success by DMs, link taps and intro sign-ups, not likes.
- **Compliance:** No health or outcome claims, direct or through testimonials. 16 CFR 255.2 means a client's story implies typical results. Never write, buy or gate reviews: the Consumer Review Rule (16 CFR 465) has been in force since October 21, 2024, and the FTC sent warning letters to 10 companies in December 2025. Any creator who gets free classes or pay must say "#ad" or similar in their own caption. Instagram's Paid Partnership label alone isn't enough.

## Key Findings (with evidence strength)

**Evidence scale used throughout:** **Strong** means official Instagram/Meta or FTC statements, regulations, or multiple large datasets that agree. **Moderate** means one large vendor dataset, or consistent findings with methodological caveats. **Anecdotal** means practitioner experience, vendor blogs or folklore. It is useful as a hypothesis to test, not as fact.

### What Instagram itself has said (Strong)
- **Ranking signals.** In January 2025 Adam Mosseri said "the top three signals that matter most for ranking are watch time, likes and sends." He told creators to watch "average watch time, likes per reach, and sends per reach." He added that likes matter slightly more with followers and sends slightly more with non-followers. Comments and saves were not named in that summary. Saves still matter as a usefulness signal, but the widely repeated claim that "saves are weighted 3× likes" is folklore with no Instagram source.
- **Carousel second chance.** Mosseri: "If someone sees your carousel post but they don't swipe, we'll often give that carousel a second chance and automatically move to that second piece of media for the viewer." Slide 2 is effectively a second cover.
- **Carousel length.** Carousels can hold up to 20 photos or videos (Mosseri, August 2024). Mosseri has also recommended carousels for reach, and said carousels with music can appear in the Reels tab.
- **Image shape.** Since January 2025 the profile grid shows posts as 3:4 tiles. Since May 2025 the feed accepts native 3:4 (1080×1440) photos. Design covers so the key text survives the 3:4 grid crop.
- **Engagement bait.** "Comment YES" or "tag 3 friends" prompts are a risk. Meta has publicly demoted engagement bait (Facebook policy since 2017). Instagram's own Recommendation Guidelines listed it until early 2025, and archived versions suggest the explicit wording was dropped around March–April 2025. That makes the Instagram penalty **Moderate** evidence. The audience penalty is clearer: in Sprout Social's Q1 2026 pulse survey of 2,250 users, 23% named "engagement-bait tactics" as the one thing they wish brands would stop.

### Format benchmarks (Moderate to Strong; metrics differ by vendor)
- **Socialinsider** (2025 data; follower-based engagement counting likes and comments only): carousels 0.55%, Reels 0.52%, images 0.37%. Socialinsider's 2026 Instagram Benchmarks (35M posts, February 20, 2026) put images at 0.45% in 2024 and 0.37% in 2025, a decline it describes as "Static images continue to decline… (17% YoY decrease)," while overall engagement was down about 24% year on year. In Socialinsider's Q3 2026 update, carousels "maintained [their] leading position, with an average of 0.50%," ahead of Reels at 0.48%; images rose from 0.33% to 0.35%, and the platform average was 0.45%. Socialinsider also reports that carousels lead on saves and that carousels with more than 10 slides got more reach.
- **Buffer** (4M+ posts, 2022–2024): Reels earned 36% more reach than carousels and 125% more than single images. Carousels earned 12% more engagement than Reels and 114% more than single images. In Buffer's larger 52M-post dataset, carousels had a 6.9% median engagement rate by reach, vs 4.4% for single images and 3.3% for Reels.
- **Rival IQ 2025:** Rival IQ's 2025 Social Media Industry Benchmark Report puts Health & Beauty's median Instagram engagement at 0.14% per post (interactions ÷ followers, across 150 companies), noting that "A saturated market hurt Health & Beauty, which had the lowest engagement across platforms." It was also the one industry where **photos** earned the highest engagement rates. For a wellness brand, good single photos are not obsolete.
- **What this means for NUMA:** the engagement edge of carousels is real but modest on follower-based metrics. Its practical value is depth: time spent, saves, sends and the second-chance impression. Reach to strangers still comes mostly from Reels.

### Carousel design (mix of Strong and Anecdotal)
- **Slide count.** The 20-slide limit is fact. Socialinsider's ">10 slides get more reach" is Moderate evidence. The common "8–10 is optimal" and "keep text under 20–50 words per slide" rules are Anecdotal, though consistent across practitioner sources. NUMA default: 6–10 slides for education, 3–5 for reveals and offers, 10–15 only when every slide earns its place.
- **First slide.** One promise in 8–12 large words, a human or space image where possible, and no logo competing with the hook. Treat slide 2 as an independent hook, which follows from the Mosseri quote above (Strong).
- **Educational vs promotional.** Sprout Social's Q1 2026 survey found educational posts about products or services are the content people most want from brands (40%), followed by community content (27%), behind-the-scenes (19%) and front-line employees (16%). Sprout's November 2026 pulse found half of consumers want brands to stop sounding "salesy and corporate." Older Sprout surveys found "too many promotional posts" drove unfollows: 35% in 2019, and in 2016, "Too many promotional messages… cause 46% of people to unfollow a brand." Those surveys are dated but point in the same direction (Moderate).
- **"Save this" / "Send this."** Asking for a save or send is fine when the content is genuinely reference-worthy (a checklist, a schedule, a what-to-bring list). Because sends are a named ranking signal, "send this to the friend you'd come with" is the stronger ask for reaching new people. Generic "SAVE THIS!!" on non-reference content is folklore and reads as bait.

### Single photos (Moderate)
- Faces: a Georgia Tech/Yahoo study of 1.1M Instagram photos found photos with faces were 38% more likely to get likes and 32% more likely to get comments. The study dates from 2014, and a Hootsuite test on one account found no face advantage, so treat it as **Moderate**. The "posting too much lowers per-post feedback" finding from the same study supports quality over volume.
- Rival IQ's Health & Beauty finding supports keeping good photos in the mix. Lead with real people (teachers, members who have consented) and the real room, not stock imagery.

### Offers and pricing law (Strong)
- **Testimonials (16 CFR 255.2, revised 2023).** A testimonial about a key attribute is read as typical. If you can't substantiate that it's typical, you must clearly disclose generally expected results, and "results not typical" is expressly insufficient. Testimonials can't make claims the advertiser couldn't substantiate directly. Under the FTC's 2022 Health Products Compliance Guidance, health benefit claims generally need "competent and reliable scientific evidence," usually randomized controlled trials, and that standard applies equally to claims made through testimonials. **Practical rule:** NUMA testimonials describe experience (the room, the teacher, feeling welcome), never outcomes (pain, sleep, anxiety, weight, "healing").
- **Material connections (16 CFR 255.5).** Free or discounted services count as a material connection, "regardless of whether the advertiser requires an endorsement in return." FTC's Disclosures 101 says to use plain terms ("Ad," "#ad," "Sponsored," "Paid partnership with NUMA") at the start of the caption. It also says "Don't assume that a platform's disclosure tool is good enough," so use the Paid Partnership label in addition to the creator's own words. Tags like "#sp," "#collab," "#thanks" or "ambassador" on their own are not enough.
- **Consumer Review Rule (16 CFR 465, effective October 21, 2024).** It bans fake or AI-generated reviews and testimonials, reviews from people with no real experience, incentives conditioned on a particular sentiment, undisclosed insider reviews, review suppression, and fake social-influence indicators. On December 22, 2025 the FTC announced it had warned 10 companies, which it did not name, about possible violations of the Consumer Review Rule, citing penalties of "up to $53,088 per violation" and giving recipients five days to respond. Christopher Mufarrige, Director of the FTC Bureau of Consumer Protection, said: "Fake or false consumer reviews are detrimental to consumers' ability to make accurate and informed choices." The letter template gives the example "giving a gift cards or discounts only for 5-star reviews." In 2026 the FTC went after TruHeight partly over reviews from "consumers who were offered a free product or discount in return for a five-star review" (reported by DLA Piper).
- **Fake urgency.** The FTC's 2022 report *Bringing Dark Patterns to Light* names "baseless countdown timers," "false limited time" messages and "false low stock" claims as deceptive design.
- **Price comparisons (16 CFR 233.1).** A "was $X" or "regularly $X" anchor must be a bona fide price at which the service was "openly and actively offered for sale, for a reasonably substantial period of time, in the recent, regular course of… business." Inflating a regular price to make $49 look bigger is exactly the deceptive example in the Guides.
- **Intro offers that auto-renew.** The FTC's broader "click-to-cancel" Negative Option Rule was vacated by the Eighth Circuit on July 8, 2025, on procedural grounds. The FTC sent a draft Advance Notice of Proposed Rulemaking to OIRA on January 30, 2026 and issued an ANPRM on negative option marketing on March 11, 2026, but no replacement rule has been adopted. ROSCA, the original Negative Option Rule, Section 5 and state auto-renewal laws such as California's still apply. If $49 converts into a recurring membership, state the renewal price, timing and how to cancel clearly at sign-up, and keep a short version in the post.

### Local studio economics (Anecdotal; vendor data)
- Studio-software vendor FitDegree reports intro-offer-to-membership conversion "often below 20 percent industry-wide," attributing the figure to "the 2024 BFS Network State of the Industry Report and fitdegree coaching observations." It sets a 30%+ target for profitable studios and says attending a second class "doubles conversion likelihood." Vendors recommend 10–21-day or month-long paid intros priced around 60–70% of the target package, and Mariana Tek data shows clients visit 5–6 times on average before committing. These are not independent studies, but they support one implication: Instagram's job is to bring in the *right* first-timers and get them to a second and third visit, not to maximize likes on an offer post.

## (a) Carousel Templates (12)

All copy is a starting point in NUMA's voice. Replace bracketed items with real details. Never add claims about health outcomes.

**C1 — "Your First Visit, Step by Step"**
- **Goal:** Reduce first-timer anxiety; convert followers who are curious.
- **Slides (7):** 1) Cover: real photo of the entrance + "What your first visit to NUMA actually looks like" · 2) Independent hook: "No experience needed. Here's the whole hour." · 3) Arrive 10 min early: who greets you · 4) What to wear and bring · 5) What happens in the room · 6) After class: tea, questions, no pressure · 7) "Your first month is $49. Details in caption."
- **Example copy:** Slide 2: "You don't need to be flexible, fit or 'good at this.' You just need to come as you are." Caption: "A lot of people tell us the hardest part was walking in the door. So here's exactly what happens, start to finish. New here? Your first month is $49, then [regular price]/month unless you cancel before [date/terms]. Cancel anytime from your account or at the front desk."
- **Evidence:** Educational/what-to-expect content is what consumers most want (Sprout Q1 2026, 40%). Studio vendors cite first-visit anxiety as a conversion barrier (Anecdotal).
- **When to use:** Monthly, and pinned to the profile.
- **Failure signs + fix:** Low swipe-through from slide 1 to 2 → put a person in the cover photo and shorten the headline to 8 words. Lots of saves but no DMs → add one line to the last slide: "Questions? DM us 'first visit' and a real person will answer." (Here the word triggers a genuine reply, not bait.)

**C2 — "What to Bring" Checklist (save-worthy)**
- **Goal:** Saves and sends; practical utility.
- **Slides (5):** 1) "What to bring to your first NUMA class (and what to leave at home)" · 2) Bring: water, layers · 3) We provide: mats, props, towels [verify] · 4) Leave at home: the pressure to keep up · 5) "Save this for the night before."
- **Example copy:** Slide 4: "Leave at home: the idea that you need to be good at this already."
- **Evidence:** Saves are highest on carousels (Socialinsider, Moderate). A save ask fits reference content (Anecdotal).
- **When to use:** Quarterly; before seasonal intro pushes.
- **Failure signs + fix:** Saves below your carousel median → make it more specific (actual items, actual room temperature, locker info).

**C3 — "Meet the Teacher"**
- **Goal:** Trust through people; likes from followers.
- **Slides (6):** 1) Portrait + "Meet [Name], who teaches Tuesday mornings" · 2) Candid teaching photo + one-line why they teach · 3) What their class feels like (experience language) · 4) A favorite moment at NUMA · 5) Their classes on the schedule · 6) "Come say hi."
- **Example copy:** Slide 3: "[Name]'s classes are slow, steady and full of options. Expect clear cues and plenty of room to rest."
- **Evidence:** Faces lift likes and comments (Georgia Tech, Moderate). Content from front-line employees is wanted by 16% of consumers (Sprout Q1 2026).
- **When to use:** 2× per month, rotating staff.
- **Failure signs + fix:** Likes below baseline → the photo is too posed. Use an in-class candid as the cover. Avoid credentials-as-claims (e.g., "heals injuries").

**C4 — "Three Myths About Starting" (educational, non-medical)**
- **Goal:** Sends to friends who are hesitating.
- **Slides (6):** 1) "3 things people think before their first class (that aren't true)" · 2) Myth 1: "I'm not flexible enough" · 3) Myth 2: "Everyone will be watching me" · 4) Myth 3: "I need the right gear" · 5) What's actually true at NUMA · 6) "Send this to the friend who keeps saying 'one day.'"
- **Example copy:** Slide 3: "Honestly? Everyone's focused on their own mat. Our teachers keep it that way."
- **Evidence:** Sends are a named ranking signal, especially for non-followers (Mosseri, Strong).
- **When to use:** Monthly.
- **Failure signs + fix:** Shares/sends near zero → make the CTA name a specific person ("the friend you'd come with"). Avoid physiological claims.

**C5 — "A Week at NUMA" Schedule Explainer**
- **Goal:** Help people choose a class; drive profile visits and bookings.
- **Slides (8):** 1) "Not sure which class to try? Start here." · 2) Class map by energy (gentle → active) · 3–7) One class per slide: what it is, who it's for, length · 8) "Best first classes: [X] and [Y]."
- **Example copy:** Slide 3: "Slow Flow · 60 min · Gentle pace, lots of options. A good first class."
- **Evidence:** Educational content about services is the #1 thing consumers want (Sprout). Carousels with more slides tend to get more reach (Socialinsider, Moderate).
- **When to use:** At each schedule change; once a quarter.
- **Failure signs + fix:** People drop off mid-carousel → cut to 6 slides; merge similar classes.

**C6 — "Behind the Scenes: Before We Open"**
- **Goal:** Warmth and authenticity.
- **Slides (6–8):** Photos only, sequenced from 6:15am lights on → props set out → tea brewed → first student arrives, with short captions on each.
- **Example copy:** Cover: "6:15am at NUMA, before anyone arrives."
- **Evidence:** Behind-the-scenes content is wanted by 19% of consumers (26% of Gen Z) (Sprout Q1 2026). Photo-led carousels suit wellness (Rival IQ H&B photo finding, Moderate).
- **When to use:** Monthly.
- **Failure signs + fix:** Low reach → add a trending-licensed song so the post is eligible for the Reels tab (Mosseri, Strong). Low likes → pick more human moments over empty-room shots.

**C7 — "Member Stories" (testimonial-as-experience)**
- **Goal:** Social proof without outcome claims.
- **Slides (5):** 1) Member photo (written consent) + "Why [First name] keeps coming back" · 2) How they found NUMA · 3) Their first visit, in their words · 4) What they look forward to (the people, the room, the routine) · 5) "Your first month is $49 → details in caption."
- **Example copy:** Slide 3: "'I almost left the parking lot. Then [teacher] remembered my name the second week.' — [First name], member since 2025." Caption disclosure if applicable: "[First name] received a complimentary month in thanks for sharing their story." (Required if anything of value was given.)
- **Evidence:** 16 CFR 255.2 (Strong). Testimonials must be real, from real customers, and unedited in meaning (16 CFR 465, Strong).
- **When to use:** 1–2× per month.
- **Failure signs + fix:** A member mentions pain, sleep, anxiety, weight or "healing" → don't publish that line, even as a quote. Low engagement → lead with a candid photo, not a quote card.

**C8 — "The Space Tour"**
- **Goal:** Let people picture themselves there.
- **Slides (8–10):** Entry → front desk → changing rooms → main studio (wide) → props wall → quiet corner → tea station → parking/transit tips → "See you soon."
- **Example copy:** Slide 9: "Free parking behind the building. Bus [#] stops at the corner."
- **Evidence:** Practical, specific info drives saves (Anecdotal). 3:4 frames fill the grid (Strong).
- **When to use:** Quarterly; after any renovation.
- **Failure signs + fix:** Many profile visits but few bookings → add a final "how to book" slide.

**C9 — "Intro Offer, Explained Honestly" (promotional)**
- **Goal:** Convert warm followers to the $49 intro.
- **Slides (5):** 1) "Your first month at NUMA: $49" with a real member/teacher photo · 2) "What's included" (unlimited classes / [X] classes; verify) · 3) "What happens after" (renewal price, date, how to cancel) · 4) "Who it's for: anyone new to NUMA" · 5) "How to start: link in bio or visit the front desk."
- **Example copy:** Slide 3: "After your first month, membership continues at [regular price]/month. You can cancel anytime before [renewal date] from your account. No hard sell, ever."
- **Evidence:** Clear negative-option disclosure (ROSCA and state laws, Strong). Bona fide regular price (16 CFR 233.1, Strong).
- **When to use:** No more than ~2× per month (see offer rules).
- **Failure signs + fix:** Many likes but few link taps or DMs → the offer is buried. Put the price on the cover and the terms on slide 2. Comments asking "what's the catch?" → move the renewal terms to slide 2.

**C10 — "Ask NUMA" FAQ**
- **Goal:** Answer objections; collect DMs.
- **Slides (7):** 1) "Your questions, answered" · 2–6) Real questions from DMs (pricing, parking, beginners, injuries → "talk to your doctor and tell your teacher; we'll offer options," cancellation) · 7) "Ask us anything in DMs."
- **Example copy:** Slide 4: "'I have an old injury. Can I still come?' Please check with your healthcare provider first, then let your teacher know before class. We'll offer options, and you're always welcome to rest."
- **Evidence:** Educational content is preferred (Sprout). Specific DM-driven questions are relevant to your own audience (Anecdotal).
- **When to use:** Monthly.
- **Failure signs + fix:** Few DMs → end with a specific prompt ("What would you want to know before your first class?").

**C11 — "Community Moments" Recap**
- **Goal:** Belonging; likes from followers.
- **Slides (10–15):** Photos from a workshop, potluck or anniversary, with brief captions. Last slide: next event date.
- **Example copy:** Cover: "Our first community potluck. Thank you for filling the room."
- **Evidence:** Community content is the #2 type consumers want (27%, Sprout). Photo-dump carousels with more slides tend toward more reach (Socialinsider, Moderate).
- **When to use:** After every event.
- **Failure signs + fix:** Low completion → cut to the 8 best photos and put a group shot first.

**C12 — "Slow Down Sunday" Practice Card (non-claim wellness tip)**
- **Goal:** Saves; a weekly ritual brand moment.
- **Slides (5):** 1) "A 5-minute pause for Sunday evening" · 2–4) Simple, non-medical steps (sit, notice your breath, stretch arms overhead, lights low) · 5) "Save it for next Sunday."
- **Example copy:** Slide 2: "Find a comfortable seat. Let your shoulders drop. No goal here, just a pause." (No claims that it lowers stress, cortisol or blood pressure.)
- **Evidence:** Save behavior on reference carousels (Moderate). Health claims need scientific substantiation (FTC Health Products Guidance, Strong), so frame this as an experience, not an effect.
- **When to use:** Weekly or bi-weekly series.
- **Failure signs + fix:** Saves drop over weeks → rotate formats (audio Reel version) and vary the practice.

## (b) Single-Photo Post Types (9)

Shoot in 3:4 (1080×1440). Use natural light, real people with written consent, and no heavy filters that misrepresent the space.

**P1 — Teacher Portrait (people)**
- **Goal:** Trust and familiarity.
- **Composition:** Eye-level, natural window light, the teacher in the studio with props softly visible; a genuine expression, not a pose.
- **Example copy:** "This is [Name]. If you come to Thursday's 6pm, she'll be the one handing you a blanket before you ask. 🤍"
- **Evidence:** Faces +38% likes, +32% comments (Georgia Tech, Moderate).
- **When to use:** Weekly rotation.
- **Failure signs + fix:** Lower likes per reach than your photo median → swap the posed shot for an in-action candid.

**P2 — Class in Motion (people)**
- **Goal:** Show what a class looks like.
- **Composition:** A wide shot from the back corner, mixed bodies and ages (all consenting), soft blur of movement; no one singled out.
- **Example copy:** "Tuesday 7am. Some days it's a full room, some days it's five of us. It always feels like ours."
- **Evidence:** Rival IQ shows photos lead in Health & Beauty (Moderate).
- **When to use:** Weekly.
- **Failure signs + fix:** Comments about "everyone looks so fit" → broaden who's pictured. Representation affects who feels welcome.

**P3 — The Empty Room (the space)**
- **Goal:** Calm, place-based brand memory.
- **Composition:** Golden-hour light across the floor, mats laid out, plants; shoot straight on with a symmetrical frame.
- **Example copy:** "The room, just before 5:30 class. Come in early and sit for a while if you like."
- **Evidence:** Anecdotal (aesthetic posts tend to get likes but rarely sends).
- **When to use:** 1–2× per month; good grid anchor.
- **Failure signs + fix:** Likes fine but no profile visits → add a practical line (class time, "drop-ins welcome").

**P4 — Detail / Texture (the space)**
- **Goal:** Sensory brand cues.
- **Composition:** Close-up of a tea pot, folded blankets, the bell; shallow depth of field.
- **Example copy:** "Small things we care about: warm blankets, fresh tea, a bell to begin."
- **Evidence:** Anecdotal.
- **When to use:** Filler between heavier posts.
- **Failure signs + fix:** Consistently the lowest-reach type → turn details into carousel slides instead.

**P5 — Behind the Scenes (BTS)**
- **Goal:** Authenticity; show the work behind the calm.
- **Composition:** The owner cleaning mats, a teacher planning a sequence on paper, deliveries arriving; candid.
- **Example copy:** "Sunday reset: 40 mats wiped, 12 blankets washed, one very patient dog supervising."
- **Evidence:** Behind-the-scenes demand is 19% overall, 26% for Gen Z (Sprout Q1 2026).
- **When to use:** 2× per month.
- **Failure signs + fix:** Low engagement → add a human in the frame; empty BTS reads as clutter.

**P6 — Testimonial-as-Experience Portrait**
- **Goal:** Social proof grounded in experience.
- **Composition:** The member in the space (consented), a short quote in the caption (not on the image), focused on belonging or routine.
- **Example copy:** "'It's the one hour of my week where nobody needs anything from me.' — [First name], Thursday regular. (Shared with permission. [First name] received no compensation for this post.)" Only claim "no compensation" if true; otherwise disclose what was given.
- **Evidence:** 16 CFR 255.2 and 255.5, 16 CFR 465 (Strong).
- **When to use:** 2× per month.
- **Failure signs + fix:** Any quote implying symptom relief → reject it. If it's ever unclear whether something was given, disclose.

**P7 — Welcome Desk / First Hello**
- **Goal:** First-timer reassurance.
- **Composition:** The front-desk person mid-smile handing over a mat; the entrance visible.
- **Example copy:** "First time? Tell us at the desk. We'll show you around, set you up, and check in after class."
- **Evidence:** Same reassurance logic as C1 (Anecdotal).
- **When to use:** Before and during offer weeks.
- **Failure signs + fix:** No DMs → add a direct line: "Nervous? DM us first. A real person answers."

**P8 — Offer Photo (single image + clear terms)**
- **Goal:** A low-pressure offer reminder.
- **Composition:** A warm people photo with minimal or no text overlay; the offer goes in the first caption line.
- **Example copy:** "New to NUMA? Your first month is $49. After that, membership is [regular price]/month unless you cancel before [date]. Book through the link in our bio, or just ask at the desk."
- **Evidence:** Price and renewal clarity (Strong legal). Single images get the least reach (Buffer), so use this as a reminder, not the main offer vehicle.
- **When to use:** At most 1× per month.
- **Failure signs + fix:** Low reach → expected. Move the offer into a carousel (C9) or Reel, and use this only in Stories or the highlight.

**P9 — Neighborhood / Local Context**
- **Goal:** Local discovery and relevance.
- **Composition:** The NUMA storefront with the street, a collaboration with a local café (if paid or bartered, disclose), a seasonal street scene.
- **Example copy:** "Find us next to [café] on [street]. Post-class coffee is a tradition here."
- **Evidence:** Anecdotal. Location tags help local search (folklore, untested).
- **When to use:** Monthly.
- **Failure signs + fix:** If a partner gets free classes for posting about NUMA, their post needs "#ad" or "Paid partnership," per FTC Disclosures 101.

| 6 | **Scarcity:** Only state capacity limits that are true and current ("our Saturday 9am fills most weeks; book ahead"). Never write "only 3 spots left" unless it's literally true at posting time. | Strong | FTC dark patterns report (false low stock / false high demand). |
| 7 | **Price anchoring:** "Normally $[X]" only if $[X] is a bona fide price NUMA has openly charged for a substantial recent period. Otherwise say "$49 for your first month, then $[X]/month." | Strong | 16 CFR 233.1. |
| 8 | **Renewal terms in the post:** If $49 auto-renews, the caption (and the sign-up page) must state the renewal price, when it starts and how to cancel, before payment. | Strong | ROSCA, the original Negative Option Rule and state auto-renewal laws (e.g., California) still apply after the July 8, 2025 vacatur of click-to-cancel. The FTC issued an Advance Notice of Proposed Rulemaking on negative option marketing on March 11, 2026; no replacement rule has been adopted. |
| 9 | **"Free" wording:** If you ever run "first class free," make it truly free with no hidden conditions, and don't call a permanently available offer "introductory." | Strong / Moderate | FTC Guide Concerning Use of the Word "Free" (16 CFR 251), Section 5. Studio vendors report free trials convert worse than paid intros (Anecdotal). |
| 10 | **No outcome promises:** No "transform," "detox," "heal," "relieve," "boost immunity," "lose X," or before/after body imagery. Describe the experience (classes, space, people). | Strong | FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance (2022). 16 CFR 255.2. |
| 11 | **Testimonials in offer posts:** Real members only, written consent, experience-focused. Disclose any comp ("received a complimentary month"). Never trade discounts for 5-star reviews. Never ask only happy clients for reviews. | Strong | 16 CFR 465. FTC Dec 22, 2025 warning letters to 10 unnamed companies (BCP Director Christopher Mufarrige). 16 CFR 255.5. |
| 12 | **Paid or gifted creators:** Require "#ad"/"Paid partnership with NUMA" at the start of the caption plus Instagram's Paid Partnership label, and the creator's own honest experience only. NUMA is responsible for monitoring. | Strong | FTC Disclosures 101. 16 CFR 255.5. 2023 Endorsement Guides. |
| 13 | **Don't bait:** Use "DM us 'first visit' for the schedule" only if someone genuinely answers. Skip "comment YES" and "tag 3 friends." | Moderate | Meta engagement-bait policy. 23% of users want brands to stop engagement bait (Sprout Q1 2026). |

**Wording patterns that work (FTC-safe):**
- "New to NUMA? Your first month is $49. After that it's $[X]/month, cancel anytime before [date]."
- "Come try a class. If it's not for you, no hard feelings, and no hard sell."
- "Intro pricing is for first-time members only, one per person."
- "Our 6pm classes often fill, so booking ahead helps." (Only if true.)

**Avoid:** "Limited time only!!" (no date), "Only 2 spots left!" (unverified), "Reduce stress and sleep better," "Life-changing results," "Was $199, now $49" (unless $199 is a real regular price), and any review you wrote, paid for or edited.

## (d) Metrics: Did It Work, and What to Change

Compare each post against **your own trailing median for the same format** (last 10–20 posts), not industry averages. Benchmarks vary wildly by method: Socialinsider uses likes plus comments per follower, Buffer uses engagement per reach, and Rival IQ uses median interactions per follower.

| Post type | Primary success metrics | Secondary | If it fails → change |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Educational carousel** | Saves per reach; sends/shares per reach | Average time/interaction depth, follows from post | Low saves → make it more specific and more reference-like. Low sends → add a "send to a friend who…" line. Low reach → strengthen cover and slide 2; test a Reel version. |
| **Story/community carousel** | Likes per reach (followers) | Comments, profile visits | Low likes → people-first cover, fewer slides, best photo first. |
| **Single photo** | Likes per reach; reach from non-followers | Profile visits | Below median twice in a row → switch to people/candid, or move the image into a carousel. Weak non-follower reach is normal for photos (Buffer). |
| **Offer post (any format)** | Link taps / website clicks, DMs, intro sign-ups attributed (ask "how did you hear about us?" and use UTM links) | Saves (people bookmarking to decide), unfollows in the 48h after posting | Likes but no taps → offer too buried. Put price and terms on slide 1–2. Taps but no sign-ups → fix the booking page friction. Unfollow spike → reduce offer frequency and add value posts. |
| **Business outcome** | Intro → 2nd visit rate; intro → membership conversion | Members who cite Instagram | If conversion is under about 20% (FitDegree's "industry-wide" level, citing the 2024 BFS Network State of the Industry Report; its target is 30%+), the issue is follow-through after the first visit (welcome, check-ins), not Instagram. |

Instagram's 2026 Insights additions (share rate, skip rate, views over time, per secondary reports from April 2026) make "sends per reach" easier to track. Where native share counts are available, use them as your proxy for Mosseri's sends metric.

**Decision rules:**
- After 4–6 posts of a template, keep it if it beats your format median on its primary metric at least half the time. Otherwise retire it or rework the cover.
- Change one variable at a time (cover image, cover headline, slide count, CTA).
- Don't judge an offer post by likes. A post with modest likes but 8 DMs and 3 sign-ups is a winner.

## Caveats

- **Benchmarks conflict by method.** "Carousels beat images" holds across Socialinsider, Buffer and Rival IQ's all-industry view. But Rival IQ found photos lead in Health & Beauty, and Ordinal's impression-based dataset found images ahead. Trust your own account data over any single benchmark.
- **Mosseri's statements describe signals, not weights.** Claims such as "a send is worth 5× a like" or "saves count 3×" are not from Instagram.
- **Slide-count and text-density rules are mostly practitioner folklore**, apart from the 20-slide limit, Socialinsider's >10-slide reach finding and the slide-2 re-show. Test them.
- **The faces study is from 2014**, and a small Hootsuite test didn't replicate it.
- **Studio conversion figures come from software vendors** with a commercial interest. They are directionally useful, not independently verified.
- **This is not legal advice.** State laws (auto-renewal, consumer protection) vary. Have counsel review the $49 terms and sign-up flow, especially if NUMA offers any licensed or medical-adjacent services (e.g., massage therapy, med-spa treatments), where state professional advertising rules may add requirements.
- **Engagement-bait policy on Instagram is in flux.** Meta's demotion rules clearly cover Facebook. Instagram's explicit guideline language appears to have changed in 2025, though avoiding bait is still right for audience trust.
