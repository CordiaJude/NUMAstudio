# R2 Formats and mix: NUMA Wellness Collective: Instagram Post Mix, Cadence, Timing and Monthly Rhythm (2025–2026 Evidence Review)

> Deep-research result for prompt R2, received 2026-11-13. Orchestrator review notes: docs/research/REVIEW.md.

For an account under 5,000 followers, NUMA should post 3–5 feed posts a week. Reels should be the main tool for reaching new local people, carousels should do the educating, saving and converting, and 1–3 Stories a day should hold on to existing members. Timing matters far less than whether people watch, send and save the content.

## TL;DR
- **Format and mix:** Make roughly 55–60% of feed posts Reels, 30–35% carousels and about 10% single photos, plus 1–3 Stories on most days and 1–2 Trial Reels a week once eligible. Every large dataset agrees that Reels reach the most people on small accounts. For accounts under 1,000 followers, Metricool/HypeAuditor's 700M-post study found median reach of 134 for Reels, 56 for carousels and 23 for images. Carousels lead on engagement and saves.
- **Cadence:** Post 3–5 feed posts a week, at most 1 a day, and avoid dark weeks. Buffer's August 2025 study of 2.1M posts from 102K accounts found that 3–5 posts a week lifted reach per post by about 12% and more than doubled the follower growth rate compared with 1–2 a week. Weeks with no posts sat below each account's own baseline.
- **Timing and calendar:** Default to Tuesday–Thursday late morning, midday or early evening Central time. The big studies disagree on exact hours (Buffer: Thursday 9 a.m.; Later: 5 a.m.; Sprout: Tuesday/Wednesday afternoons), which is why NUMA's own data has to decide. Put offers in the first 5 days of the month and around the 15th, start the January push on December 26, and build summer content around Mobile's July–August heat peak (NOAA: about 22 days of 90°F+ in each month).

## Key Findings

1. **Reels are how a small account reaches non-followers, and that holds at NUMA's size.** Under 50K followers, Reels lead median reach in every tier of the Metricool/HypeAuditor Playbook (700M posts, 28M influencer accounts, January–June 2025). Buffer's 4M+ post analysis found Reels got 36% more reach than carousels and 125% more than single images. Socialinsider's 2025 benchmark says Reels from accounts under 5K followers average about 300 views, roughly a 20% view rate.
2. **Carousels win on engagement and saves.** Socialinsider (2025 data, 35M posts from 447,613 brand accounts) measured engagement rate by followers at 0.55% for carousels, 0.52% for Reels and 0.37% for images, with image engagement down about 18% year over year. Buffer measures engagement against reach instead and gets 6.9% for carousels, 4.4% for images and 3.3% for Reels. Metricool's 2026 study (24.4M posts, 375K accounts) found carousels earn 9x more saves than single images.
3. **Sources disagree on reach.** Socialinsider's newer reach-rate data (872,075 brand posts, January 2025–August 2026) puts carousels slightly ahead: 4.50% versus 4.10% for Reels and 4.00% for images. That is a brand-account, follower-based measure, while Buffer and Metricool measure all reach including non-followers. For an account under 5K whose goal is new local customers, the Reels-first evidence is more relevant, but NUMA should test this on its own account (Experiment 2).
4. **Instagram's stated ranking signals are watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach.** Adam Mosseri said on January 22, 2025 that "the top three signals that matter most for ranking are watch time, likes and sends," adding that likes count slightly more with followers and sends slightly more with non-followers. Claims that a send is "worth 3–5x a like" are agency extrapolation; Mosseri's own wording is "slightly more important."
5. **The posting frequency evidence is the strongest in this review.** Buffer's frequency study compares each account against itself, which controls for big accounts naturally posting more. It found follower growth of +0.12% a week at 1–2 posts, +0.26% at 3–5, +0.44% at 6–9 and +0.66% at 10+, with reach per post about 12%, 18% and 24% above the 1–2 baseline respectively. Returns shrink at each step. Dash Social (3.3M posts, 3,000+ brands) found engagement rate per post peaks at 2–3 feed posts a week. There is no real conflict: posting more grows total reach and followers, while the rate per post can dip.
6. **Stories keep existing followers; they do not find new ones.** Socialinsider's 2025 Stories benchmark (160K+ Stories, 46,500 brand accounts) found accounts with 1K–5K followers reach about 9.5–10.4% of followers per Story and post about 12 Stories a month. Cumulative reach rose from 6.3% of followers with one Story to 37.8% at 13. Dash Social puts average Story completion at about 70%.
7. **Changes in 2025–2026 that alter the playbook:**
   - Trial Reels (December 10, 2024) show a Reel only to non-followers first. Meta's June 2025 analysis of 400,000+ creators found 40% posted Reels more often after trying them, and 80% of those saw more non-follower reach. Scheduling was added on April 2, 2026.
   - Instagram capped hashtags at 5 per post or Reel on December 18, 2025. Announcing the cap, Mosseri said "a few specific tags actually perform better than a long list of generic ones," and that hashtags help with search but don't necessarily increase a post's reach (Digital Trends, December 19, 2025).
   - Since July 10, 2025, public posts from professional accounts can be indexed by Google, so captions now work as search copy.
   - A Skip Rate metric (share of views skipped in the first 3 seconds) arrived in August 2025.

| C1 | Post **3–5 feed posts a week** (Reels + carousels + photos). Don't "make up" with a burst.** Reach dips after a pause because the system has stale data about the account; there is no evidence of a punitive penalty. | C6 | **Rate per post can fall as volume rises; judge by weekly totals and bookings.** Dash Social: engagement rate per post peaks at 2–3 feed posts a week. | Measured | Dash Social (3.3M posts, 3,000+ brands, Jul 2025–Jun 2026) | Medium | Report weekly reach and bookings, not only engagement rate per post. |
| C7 | **When a post outperforms, follow up within 1–2 days** with a related piece. | Official (Mosseri commentary, as reported) | Mosseri, via secondary reports | Low-medium | Note each post above 2x the median reach; publish a follow-up within 48h and compare its reach with the median. |

**Eligibility note:** Reports conflict. Trial Reels were opened in July 2025 to public accounts with 1,000+ followers, but the Help Center now lists 200 followers for professional accounts and 1,000 for personal ones. NUMA should check for the "Trial" toggle on its professional account.

## 3. Timing (US Central time)

**What the big studies say.** All of them report times in the audience's local time, so they apply directly in Central time.

| Study | Sample and dates | Best slots | Worst |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buffer (State of Social Engagement 2026) | 9.6M Instagram posts, 200K+ accounts, Jan 2024–Dec 2025 | Thursday 9 a.m., Wednesday 12 p.m., Wednesday 6 p.m.; Wednesday best day | Friday and Saturday; 1–5 a.m. |
| Buffer (earlier study) | 2M+ posts | Weekdays 3 p.m. and 6 p.m.; Friday best | — |
| Sprout Social | About 2B engagements, about 307K profiles, Nov 2025–Feb 2026 (cross-network) | Tuesday 1–7 p.m., Wednesday 12–9 p.m.; healthcare: Monday 12–9 p.m., Wednesday 11 a.m.–5 p.m. | Weekends |
| Later | 6M+ feed posts, Jan–Oct 2024; 975K Reels | 5 a.m.; Monday best day; Reels: midnight | Saturday |
| Hootsuite | 1M+ social posts (with Critical Truth), 118 countries | Monday, Tuesday, Thursday; early morning and early evening | — |

**How to read this.**
- The studies **contradict each other on hours and on Friday**. Buffer's newer data names Friday a weak day, while its earlier study named Friday the best.
- They **agree on midweek** (Tuesday–Thursday) and that **late night and Saturday are weak**.
- Industry "fitness & wellness: 5–7 a.m. and 5–8 p.m." tables circulate on agency blogs without published methodology. Treat them as **opinion**.
- For a local service business, NUMA's followers are almost all in Central time and their routine probably follows its class times. A plausible pattern is pre-work (6–8 a.m.), lunch (11 a.m.–1 p.m.) and post-work (5–8 p.m.), and NUMA should test it.

**How much timing matters.** Timing ranks well below content quality.
- Mosseri's named ranking signals (watch time, likes per reach, sends per reach) do not include posting time.
- Recency affects ordering in followers' feeds but does not decide recommendations to non-followers.
- Buffer's own conclusion is that "a steady schedule at decent times is a safer bet than rare posts at perfect times."
- The gaps between slots in these studies are small compared with the gaps between a good and a weak Reel.
- Timing matters most for **Stories and time-sensitive posts** (same-day openings, events), which live for 24h and go mostly to followers.

**How to find NUMA's own best times (method):**
1. Check Insights → Followers → **Most active times** by day and hour. Pick the 3 peak windows and post **30–60 minutes before** each peak (Mosseri's guidance, as reported by Buffer).
2. Rotate posts across 3 slots: for example 7 a.m., 12 p.m. and 6 p.m., spread over Tuesday–Thursday plus one weekend slot. Keep the format mix the same in every slot.
3. Measure **reach at 48h and at 7 days, divided by followers at posting time**, plus sends and saves per reach. Use the median, not the mean, because one viral Reel distorts averages.
4. **Minimum data:** at least 8–10 posts per slot of the same format, over 6–8 weeks, before acting. Under 5K followers, individual posts vary a lot, so with fewer than about 8 per slot any difference under 25–30% is probably noise. This threshold is a statistical rule of thumb (opinion), not a published standard.
5. Re-check every quarter. Seasonal schedules (summer heat, school calendar) shift when people are active.

**Seasonal anchors for a Gulf Coast recovery studio:**
- **New Year (strongest measured seasonal effect).**
  - IHRSA data puts 12% of annual US gym sign-ups in January, against an average of 8.3% in each other month (as reported by WITN, December 2023).
  - GymMaster's 2025 survey gives January 10.6% of US sign-ups, with a second bump in May.
  - Bloomberg Second Measure (2017–2018 data) found January new customers 79% above the average month at Planet Fitness and 53% at Pure Barre. Boutique studios also show a January spike, though smaller than budget gyms.
  - Plan: start teaser content and the January offer on **December 26–31**, push hardest **January 1–10**, then switch to retention content (habit, "how members stick with it") from **late January through mid-February**. Industry blogs say many resolution joiners drop off by mid-February; treat that date as observed, not measured.
- **Heat season (Mobile, Alabama).**
  - NOAA 1991–2020 normals for Mobile Regional Airport: **77.5 days a year at 90°F or above**. By month: May 5.1, June 15.7, **July 22.0, August 21.6**, September 11.8.
  - The median first 90°F day is about May 23 and the last about September 29 (derived from NOAA daily data by a third party).
  - NWS Mobile/Pensacola issues a heat advisory when the heat index is expected to reach 108–112°F or the air temperature 103–104°F, and an Extreme Heat Warning at a heat index of 113°F+ or air temperature of 105°F+ (NBC15, citing NWS Mobile/Pensacola). Dated advisories (June 17, 2022; June 26, 2023; June 29–30, 2026) suggest an advisory season from **mid/late June through August**. That is an inference from individual events, not an official NWS statistic.
  - Plan: start "summer cold plunge / heat recovery" education in **mid-May**. Run the main cold-plunge offer in **late June–August**. Use **heat-advisory days as same-day Story triggers**, such as "plunge after work today" with booking links. Shift sauna-led content to **October–March**, when sauna is more appealing.
- **Other local hooks (opinion, low confidence).** Mobile's Mardi Gras season in February–March suits "parade-week recovery" content. Hurricane season (June 1–November 30) calls for pre-written "open/closed" Story templates. September's routine reset is reported by gym-industry blogs as a secondary sign-up bump.

## 5. Starting default schedule for NUMA

**This is a hypothesis to test, not a proven optimum.** It assumes about 4 feed posts a week, Stories on most days and 1–2 Trial Reels a week. All times are Central.

**Standing weekly skeleton:**
- **Tuesday, 11:30 a.m.:** Education carousel (save/send-oriented, keyword-rich first caption line such as "cold plunge Mobile AL").
- **Wednesday, 6:00 p.m.:** Reel (hook in the first 3s; aim for something worth sending to a friend).
- **Thursday, 9:00 a.m.:** Reel or Trial Reel promoted to the feed.
- **Saturday or Sunday, 9:00 a.m.:** Community post (Reel or carousel; a single photo is fine). Weekend is officially weak, but local leisure browsing is worth testing.
- **Trial Reels:** 1–2 a week (Monday and Friday), testing hooks and topics.
- **Stories:** 1–3 on most days, covering today's openings, polls, reshares of the latest feed post with a booking link, and member moments.
- **Hashtags:** 3–5 specific tags per post (for example local + service + intent).
- **Captions:** search-friendly; posts can now appear in Google results.

| Week | Tuesday carousel | Wednesday Reel | Thursday Reel | Weekend post | Stories focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Week 1 (days 1–7)** | Offer explainer: "What your first visit looks like + this month's intro deal" | Hook-led Reel: "3 signs your body needs a recovery day" with CTA to the offer | Testimonial or first-timer reaction Reel (Trial-tested first) | Community: meet a practitioner | Offer launch countdown, link sticker, FAQ poll |
| **Week 2 (days 8–14)** | Education: "Sauna vs cold plunge: which, when, how long" | Event teaser Reel (7–10 days out) | Myth-busting Reel | Member spotlight | Event RSVP sticker, Q&A box |
| **Week 3 (days 15–21)** | Mid-month offer reminder carousel (payday window) + event reminder | Event day Reel / live Stories | Education Reel: "Contrast therapy protocol in 30 seconds" | Event recap Reel (within 48h) | Mid-month offer reminder, event countdown |
| **Week 4 (days 22–end)** | Retention carousel: "How members build a weekly recovery habit" | Behind-the-scenes Reel (cleaning, temperatures, the room) | Seasonal Reel (heat/New Year/Mardi Gras, as relevant) + next-month teaser | Community or local partner collab | Next-month preview, "book next week" prompts |

## 6. Five experiments for the first 60 days

Measure everything at 48h and 7 days. Log reach, reach to non-followers, average watch time, skip rate, sends per reach, saves per reach, profile visits, link clicks, and bookings tagged by source (ask "how did you hear about us" and use a UTM or promo code for each offer).

1. **Hook test with Trial Reels.** Each week, publish 2 Trial Reels with the same body and different first-3-second hooks: a question, a visual surprise or a bold claim. Promote the winner to the feed.
   - Success metric: skip rate and non-follower reach.
   - Duration: 8 weeks (about 16 trials).
   - Why: Mosseri calls the first 3 seconds "super important" but names no winning hook type, so NUMA has to find its own.
2. **Reel versus carousel on the same topic.** Take 4 education topics and publish each as a Reel and as a carousel, about a week apart.
   - Compare reach, non-follower reach, saves, sends and profile visits.
   - Why: this settles the Buffer/Metricool versus Socialinsider disagreement for NUMA's audience and sets its real format mix.
3. **Time-slot rotation.** Rotate the Wednesday and Thursday posts across 7 a.m., 12 p.m. and 6 p.m. Central for 8 weeks, holding format constant, and add one weekend 9 a.m. slot.
   - Judge only after 8 or more posts per slot, using median reach per follower at 7 days.
4. **Frequency: 3 versus 5 feed posts a week.** Run weeks 1–4 at 3 a week and weeks 5–8 at 5 a week (or alternate fortnights to control for seasonality).
   - Compare weekly follower growth, total weekly reach and bookings.
   - Why: tests whether Buffer's 3–5 finding holds for NUMA and whether the extra posts are worth the production time.
5. **Offer timing: the 1st versus the 15th.** Launch the same intro offer with a unique code once in days 1–5 and once in days 14–16, with matched Story support. Compare redemptions and cost in staff time.
   - Why: the payday spending effect is measured in consumer data, but its effect on Instagram-driven bookings has not been tested.

## Caveats
- **Vendor data.** Buffer, Later, Sprout, Hootsuite, Metricool and Socialinsider all study their own customers, and each publishes partly to market its scheduler. Samples lean toward active brand and creator accounts, and none segments local wellness studios specifically.
- **Correlation and definitions.** Buffer's frequency study is correlational, though it does compare each account with itself. The engagement-rate definitions differ (by followers versus by reach), which explains most of the apparent conflicts.
- **Sources disagree on:**
  - Reach leadership: Reels (Buffer, Metricool) versus carousels (Socialinsider's newer reach rate).
  - Best hours: Buffer versus Later versus Sprout.
  - Friday: Buffer's earlier versus newer studies.
  - Trial Reels eligibility thresholds: 200 versus 1,000 followers.
- **Meta's own Trial Reels figures** are self-reported and come from creators who chose to use the feature.
- **Case studies are agency self-reports and are not independently verified.**
  - Therma Haus, a Denver sauna and cold-plunge studio, reported 3,500+ profile visits and 550+ followers in July–October 2026. Those results came from **paid** Meta ads, not organic posting, and Meta and Google booking counts may overlap.
  - An unnamed Manchester yoga studio grew from about 400 to about 2,400 followers in 3 months on 3 feed posts a week plus 2 short Reels a week. Its Reels reached 1,500–4,000 accounts, mostly non-followers.
  - JAG Medical Spa (about 2,200 followers) raised monthly Reel views from under 10,000 to over 45,000 in May–December 2025 with on-camera voiceover Reels.
  - Treat all three as **observed** illustrations, not proof.
- **Unsupported claims to ignore.** Hashtag-count tricks, "sends are worth 15 likes," and exact "best hour for wellness" tables have no published methodology; treat them as opinion.
- **Heat-advisory season** is inferred from dated events, not an NWS climatology.
