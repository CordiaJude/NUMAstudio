# R3 Script structures: Instagram Reels Script & Edit Playbook for NUMA Wellness Collective (2024–2026 evidence)

> Deep-research result for prompt R3, received 2026-11-13. Orchestrator review notes: docs/research/REVIEW.md. Note: several example beats assume amenities NUMA may not have (robes, tea lounge, lockers, 60-minute sessions); the scriptwriter must replace them with NUMA's real facts from brand.json.

For NUMA, the Reels that keep people watching and get sent or saved are the ones that win the first 3 seconds with a visual and a text hook, run about 20–60 seconds at roughly 2.5–3 spoken words per second with a visible change every 2–4 seconds, are captioned for sound-off viewing, and end on a specific reason to send the Reel to a particular person or save it for a particular moment. Generic asks ("share this!", "tag 3 friends") work against you. Instagram's own head has named watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach as the three signals that matter most. Sends weigh slightly more when a Reel is shown to non-followers, which is how a local business finds new customers. So every structure below has one job: cut skips and earn sends, while making no health-outcome claims.

## TL;DR

- **Optimize for three official signals.** Watch time (fewest skips in 0–3 s, flattest retention line), sends per reach (for non-follower reach) and likes per reach (for followers). Adam Mosseri named these on January 22, 2025; Buffer's 2026 algorithm guide summarizes his advice as tracking "the percent of viewers who liked a reel, the percent who sent it to a friend, and average watch time." Instagram replaced "view rate" with "skip rate" in August 2025 so creators can diagnose the hook directly.
- **Length depends on the goal, and the big studies disagree.** Socialinsider's ~9.8M-Reel H1 2026 study puts the best engagement (0.35%) and median views (10,374) at 45–60 s. Buffer's 2.7M-Reel 2026 study found 90–179 s Reels got +7.4% reach against each account's own baseline, with little difference below 90 s. Both agree that anything over 3 minutes loses non-follower reach. Default to 7–15 s for loops and reach, 20–45 s for sends and offers, 45–90 s for saves, founder stories and recaps.
- **For a wellness business, "FTC-safe" is a design constraint, not a disclaimer.** The FTC's 2022 Health Products Compliance Guidance requires competent and reliable scientific evidence (generally randomized controlled human trials) for health-benefit claims. Its Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (effective October 21, 2024) allows civil penalties for fake or undisclosed-insider testimonials. NUMA's Reels should sell the experience, the ritual, the people and the event, never outcomes like "recovery," "detox," "inflammation" or "immunity."

## Key Findings

**1. What the algorithm rewards (official statements)**

- Mosseri, January 22, 2025: "The top three signals that matter most for ranking are watch time, likes and sends… Likes are slightly more important for connected content, and sends are slightly more important for unconnected content." *Evidence: official statement.*
- Mosseri has said Instagram looks at both the percentage of a video watched and the seconds watched: "If you watch 10 seconds of a minute long video, that's just as many seconds as if it was 10 seconds of a 10 second video, so you won't be penalized." *Evidence: official statement.* Implication: a longer Reel is not punished for being long. It is punished for being boring.
- Mosseri's 2023 ranking explainer lists the Reels actions Instagram predicts: resharing, watching to the end, liking, and visiting the audio page. Meta's Reels system card adds a prediction of "watch less than three seconds." *Evidence: official statement.*
- Instagram says it shows Reels less often when they are low-resolution, watermarked, muted, bordered, mostly text, or already posted on Instagram. The creator FAQ adds clickbait, engagement bait and contest or giveaway promotion as limits on distribution. *Evidence: official statement.*
- Reels can be up to 20 minutes (Help Center), but "Reels over 3 minutes won't be recommended to new audiences." The cap was raised from 90 seconds to 3 minutes in January 2025. Mosseri added that Instagram is "still focused on short form video over long form." *Evidence: official statement.*
- Trial Reels are shown only to non-followers first. Mosseri advises comparing them only against other Trial Reels. *Evidence: official statement.* This is NUMA's cleanest built-in way to A/B test hooks.

**2. What the new Insights metrics measure (official)**

- In August 2025 Instagram added a Retention chart and replaced view rate with skip rate: "the percentage of views from people who decided to skip your reel during those first 3 seconds." On retention: "you'll always see a downward slope, but the flatter the line, the more engaged your audience is." *Evidence: official statement.*
- Social Media Today (April 26, 2026) reported Instagram's announcement of an improved Insights UI that "added more insights, including share and skip rate percentage data," and noted that collab posts, trial reels, cross-posted content and boosted content are not included in the new display. *Evidence: trade-press reporting of an official announcement, not an Instagram primary page.*
- Instagram publishes no "good" skip-rate benchmark. Practitioners loosely call under ~30–40% healthy and over ~50% a hook problem. *Evidence: practitioner consensus, thin.* Benchmark against your own median across 10–20 Reels.

**3. Length (large-sample studies, conflicting)**

| Length | Socialinsider ER (9.77M Reels, brands 1K–1M followers, H1 2026) | Socialinsider median views | Buffer reach vs. account's own baseline (2.7M Reels, Jan 1–Aug 15, 2026) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–30 s | 0.28% | 4,700 | Below 90 s: "differences small enough to ignore" |
| 30–45 s | 0.30% | 8,564 | (same) |
| 45–60 s | **0.35%** | **10,374** | (same) |
| 60–90 s | 0.30% | 9,790 | (same) |
| 90–120 s | 0.30% | 8,000 | **+7.4% (90–179 s bucket)** |
| 120–180 s | 0.33% | 9,000 | (same bucket) |
| 180 s+ | 0.15% | 4,428 | −49.3% |

How to read this: Socialinsider's engagement rate counts only likes and comments per follower, because shares and saves are private. It therefore says nothing directly about sends or saves. Buffer controls for account size by comparing each Reel with its own account's baseline, which is methodologically stronger for reach. Both agree on the 3-minute cliff. Neither study is specific to wellness or local businesses.

**4. Format context (large-sample studies)**

- Buffer (4M+ posts, January 2022–October 2024): Reels got 36% more reach than carousels and 125% more than single images. Carousels got 12% more engagement than Reels. *Evidence: large-sample study.* Use Reels for discovery and carousels for deep saves.
- Metricool 2026 (24.36M posts from 375,118 accounts): Reels generated more than 4× the interactions of single-image posts, and roughly 65% of a Reel's views and 61% of its interactions arrive in the first 72 hours. *Evidence: large-sample study.* Judge a Reel at 72 hours, not at 2 hours.
- Socialinsider engagement report (21.3M posts, October 2025–March 2026): Reels had the highest share rate at 0.10% of followers and a 0.04% save rate per follower. Median per Reel was 35 saves and 5 shares. *Evidence: large-sample study, per-follower denominators.*
- Dash Social (3.3K+ global brands, H2 2025), Wellness row: average reach 37K, 388 shares, 282 saves. That works out to roughly **10.5 sends and 7.6 saves per 1,000 reached**, against roughly 7.1 and 3.4 across all industries. *Evidence: large-sample study. The per-1K ratios are derived by dividing averages and skew toward large brands; they are not a published benchmark.* Treat ~10 sends/1K and ~7–8 saves/1K as a stretch goal for a small local account, not a pass/fail line.
- Rival IQ's 2025 benchmark put the Health & Beauty median Instagram engagement rate at 0.14% per follower, against an all-industry median of 0.36%. *Evidence: large-sample study, brand accounts.* Low baseline engagement is normal in this vertical.

**5. Pacing, text and sound (mixed evidence, thinnest area)**

- **Words per second.** Conversational US English runs about 150 wpm, or 2.5 words per second (National Center for Voice and Speech). Practitioners recommend 150–180 wpm (2.5–3.0 wps) for short-form. One creator analysis reported that above-median Reels ran 2.76–3.4 wps and went quiet during demonstrations. *Evidence: anecdotal.* A 1,290-video YouTube comparison found hits and flops at nearly identical pace (median 181 vs. 180 wpm), so within normal ranges, pace alone does not separate winners. *Evidence: medium-sample study, different platform.* Conclusion: cut words, not speed. Aim for 2.3–3.0 wps and leave silent beats for visual payoffs.
- **Cut frequency.** No rigorous published study links average shot length or cuts per second to Reels retention. A June 2026 arXiv study of 9,654 brand TikTok videos found AI-labelled "editing pace" among the features most associated with likes, comments, shares and saves. But pace was a categorical label with moderate annotation reliability (α = 0.45), not a measured cut rate. Specific "one cut every 1.5–2.5 s" claims circulating online are unsourced. *Evidence: thin.* Working rule (practitioner consensus): a visible change every 2–4 seconds (cut, punch-in, new text line, angle change, or action) and never more than ~4 s of static framing.
- **Sound-off.** According to Buffer's 2026 algorithm guide, "Mosseri told Colin and Samir that about half of Instagram videos are watched without sound." This is a secondhand account of an interview remark, not published data. The Verizon Media/Publicis study (n = 5,616 US consumers, 2019) found 69% watch with sound off in public, and 80% are more likely to finish a video with captions. The oft-cited "85% watch without sound" figure is old Facebook-feed data. Some sources claim the opposite for Reels: Cropink's 2026 Reels statistics page says "Most Reels (80%) are watched with sound," without citing a primary source. *Evidence: conflicting.* Whatever the true share, captions plus a text hook make the Reel work either way.
- **On-screen text.** Instagram's Creators guidance says to hook within the first 3 seconds and design for sound-off viewing, and its ranking explainer says Reels that are mostly text get shown less. Fanpage Karma's 1,000-post study found text-overlay posts drew 40% more engagement, but posts without text drew 60% more interactions per view. *Evidence: small-sample study, conflicting.* One practitioner scan found 75 of 79 outlier Reels had text in the first 3 seconds. *Evidence: anecdotal.* Rule: use one short hook line (5–8 words) plus burned-in captions, never paragraphs on screen.

**6. Calls to action that don't hurt reach (official rules plus practitioner consensus)**

- Meta's distribution guidelines demote "posts that explicitly request engagement (such as votes, shares, comments, tags, likes, or other reactions) for purposes other than a specific call to action." Meta's spam policy prohibits requiring likes, shares or follows to unlock content. Instagram's creator FAQ says contest or giveaway promotion and engagement bait can limit distribution. *Evidence: official statement.*
- Practical reading: "Send this to the friend who's been asking about Numa Nights" is a specific, value-tied suggestion. "SHARE THIS / tag 3 friends / comment YES" is bait. Comment-keyword DM automations ("comment NIGHTS for the lineup") are widely used and arguably a "specific call to action," but how Instagram classifies them is unclear. *Evidence: thin/conflicting.* Use them on offer Reels, not on Reels meant to reach non-followers.
- Put the send or save line where it is earned: right after the payoff, as on-screen text plus the first caption line. Avoid the first 3 seconds, which drives skips.

**7. FTC-safe guardrails for every NUMA Reel**

- The FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance (December 2022) states that health-benefit claims require "competent and reliable scientific evidence," generally randomized, controlled human clinical testing. Anecdotes, traditional use and observational studies are not enough. *Evidence: official regulator guidance.*
- The Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (16 CFR Part 465, effective October 21, 2024) bans fake or AI-generated testimonials, undisclosed insider reviews (staff, owners, relatives), and incentives conditioned on positive sentiment. Courts can impose civil penalties for knowing violations. The Endorsement Guides (revised 2023) require clear disclosure of material connections, such as a comped session, and testimonials must not misrepresent typical experiences. *Evidence: official regulator rule.*
- NUMA language rules:
  - **Do say:** what's included ("40-minute sauna slot, cold plunge, tea lounge"), how a space looks and sounds, schedules, prices, who it's for ("first-timers welcome"), a guest's feelings about the vibe in their own words ("it felt like a night out without the noise").
  - **Don't say:** recover faster, reduce inflammation or soreness, detox, boost immunity or metabolism, improve sleep, heal, treat, "clinically proven," or any before/after of bodies or symptoms. A guest testimonial making those claims is still NUMA's claim once NUMA posts it.
  - **Always:** disclose comped or paid guests ("Guest received a complimentary session") on screen and in the caption. Use real guests only. Add "Check with your healthcare provider if you're unsure heat or cold sessions are right for you" wherever heat or cold is shown.
  - If NUMA operates outside the US, local advertising codes (for example the UK's CAP Code) apply similar or stricter rules. This is not legal advice; have a professional review any recurring claims language.

## Details: 16 Reel structures for NUMA

*Evidence-level key: **OS** = official statement (Instagram/Meta/FTC); **LS** = large-sample study; **PC** = practitioner consensus; **AN** = anecdotal. No study tests these story arcs specifically in wellness or local-service niches. Every structure's arc-level evidence is PC or AN, built on OS and LS mechanics (skip rate, watch time, sends). Seconds are targets at ~2.5–3 wps. Burned-in captions are assumed on every structure.*

### S1 — Myth vs Fact: "What people get wrong about their first sauna/plunge visit"

- **Goal:** Saves and sends (non-follower reach).
- **Length:** 20–40 s.

| Beat | Seconds | Said | Shown |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–2 | "Three things people get wrong before their first NUMA visit." | Text: "First visit myths ❌✅". Host walks into frame at the plunge, mid-motion. |
| Myth 1 | 2–9 | "Myth: you need to be 'into fitness.' Fact: most of our guests just want a quiet hour." | Cut to a lounge with guests in robes. Red ❌ then green ✅ text. |
| Myth 2 | 9–16 | "Myth: you have to plunge. Fact: everything's optional; you set your own pace." | Guest sitting by the plunge, chatting. |
| Myth 3 | 16–24 | "Myth: you need special gear. Fact: towels, robes and tea are here." | Fast push-in on the towel shelf and tea station. |
| Payoff + send line | 24–30 | "Send this to the friend who keeps saying 'one day.'" | Text overlay of the send line over the entry door. |
| Loop tail | 30–32 | (silence) | Final frame matches the opening walk-in so it loops. |

- **Retention devices:** a numbered promise ("three"), open loop, a pattern interrupt every ~6 s, ❌/✅ visual rhythm, loop tail.
- **Share/save lines (FTC-safe):** "Send this to the friend who keeps saying 'one day' about NUMA." "Save for your first visit: robes and tea are on us."
- **Evidence:** Sends weighted for unconnected reach (**OS**, Mosseri January 2025). Numbered-list structure (**PC**). Myths must be about the *experience*, not physiology; "myth: cold plunges speed recovery" framed either way is a health claim needing RCT-level substantiation (**OS**, FTC 2022).
- **Failure signs → fix:** Skip rate above your median (or ~50%+) means the hook is abstract; open on the strongest myth said straight to camera, with text in frame 1. Retention cliff after myth 1 means the myths are too similar; reorder so the most surprising comes second. Saves fine but sends under ~5 per 1K means there's no "person" to send it to; name the recipient ("your gym-averse partner").

### S2 — Relatable POV "Send-to" sketch: "POV: your friend who 'doesn't do wellness' at Numa Nights"

- **Goal:** Sends (top unconnected-reach driver).
- **Length:** 10–20 s.

| Beat | Seconds | Said | Shown |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–2 | none (trending or original audio) | Text: "POV: you dragged your 'I don't do wellness' friend to Numa Nights". Friend with arms crossed at the door. |
| Escalation | 2–8 | none | Three quick cuts: friend sniffing the tea, hesitating at the robe, side-eyeing the playlist. |
| Turn | 8–13 | none | Friend relaxed in the lounge, asking "when's the next one?" in text. |
| Send line | 13–16 | none | Text: "Send to the friend you're bringing next time." |

- **Retention devices:** an identity hook (viewers picture a specific friend), escalation, a comic reversal, short enough to rewatch.
- **Share/save lines:** "Send to the friend you're bringing to the next Numa Nights." "Your +1 for Thursday, sorted."
- **Evidence:** Sends per reach is a top signal (**OS**). Short loops drive rewatches, and replays count as views and watch time (**OS**, Instagram help definitions). Sketch performance in local wellness is unmeasured (**AN**). Cast staff or friends who have consented. If the "friend" is staff, don't present it as a real customer testimonial (**OS**, 2024 FTC rule on insider testimonials).
- **Failure signs → fix:** High views but sends under ~5 per 1K means the premise is funny but not targetable; make the friend archetype more specific. Skip rate high means the opening frame reads as an ad; open on a face and the reaction, not the logo or the building.

### S3 — First-Timer Reaction: "Sam tries NUMA for the first time"

- **Goal:** Sends plus profile visits from non-followers.
- **Length:** 25–45 s.

| Beat | Seconds | Said | Shown |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–3 | Guest: "I've never done any of this. Okay." | Text: "First time at NUMA (real guest, unscripted)". Close-up on a nervous face. |
| Setup | 3–8 | Host: "Here's the plan: sauna, plunge if you want, tea after." | Quick walkthrough, three cuts. |
| Moment 1 | 8–18 | Guest reacts in the sauna ("okay this is… quiet"). | Steam, close-ups, real reaction. |
| Moment 2 (peak) | 18–28 | Guest approaches the plunge; countdown "3, 2, 1." | Hold for the reaction, with no cut at the climax. |
| Verdict | 28–36 | Guest, in their own words, about the *experience* ("that was way more fun than I expected"). | Lounge, tea, laugh. |
| Send line + disclosure | 36–40 | "Who would you bring?" | Text: "Send to your plunge buddy" plus "Sam received a complimentary session." |

- **Retention devices:** a stakes question ("will they do it?"), countdown, held reaction (anticipation drives watch-through), real-guest authenticity.
- **Share/save lines:** "Send this to the friend who'd scream louder than Sam." "Save this if your first NUMA visit is on the list. Here's exactly how it goes."
- **Evidence:** Authenticity is consistently ranked first in what consumers want from brands; Sprout's 2022 Index found 39% want real customer testimonials or demos (**LS** survey). The FTC requires disclosure of material connections (a comped session) and bars outcome claims in testimonials without substantiation (**OS**). Do not keep a "my soreness was gone" line in the edit.
- **Failure signs → fix:** Drop at 8–12 s (setup too long) means you should cut straight from the hook to the sauna and move the plan into on-screen text. Low completion means the peak comes too late; move the plunge countdown to ~12–15 s. Profile visits fine but no follows means the bio doesn't say what NUMA is or where it is; fix the bio line and pin this Reel.

### S4 — Explainer: "What actually happens in a 60-minute NUMA session"

- **Goal:** Saves plus profile visits (consideration).
- **Length:** 30–60 s.

| Beat | Seconds | Said | Shown |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–3 | "Here's exactly what your first 60 minutes at NUMA look like." | Text: "Your first 60 min at NUMA ⏱". A clock graphic in the corner starts at 0:00. |
| Arrival | 3–12 | "Check-in, robe, locker. Takes five minutes." | First-person walk from the door to the locker. Clock jumps to 0:05. |
| Block 1 | 12–24 | "Sauna block: stay as long as feels comfortable; staff are around." | Sauna interior, timer on the wall, clock at 0:20. |
| Block 2 | 24–36 | "Cold plunge is optional; most first-timers try a quick dip." | Plunge; text "optional." |
| Block 3 | 36–46 | "Then tea lounge, no phones, no rush." | Lounge, pour shot. |
| Save line | 46–52 | "Save this so you know the flow before you come." | Text overlay plus booking cue. |
| Safety line | 52–55 | (text only) | "Unsure if heat/cold is right for you? Check with your healthcare provider." |

- **Retention devices:** a running clock (progress bar keeps people watching to the end), chronological structure, concrete numbers.
- **Share/save lines:** "Save this before your first visit: the whole flow in 60 seconds." "Send to whoever you're booking with so they know what to bring."
- **Evidence:** Socialinsider's 45–60 s bracket leads on engagement rate and median views (**LS**, H1 2026). Buffer's reach data shows little difference below 90 s (**LS**). "Time-stamped walkthrough" is practitioner-recommended for saves (**PC**). Describe the process, not effects (**OS**, FTC).
- **Failure signs → fix:** Saves under ~3 per 1K means it reads as a promo, not a reference; add specifics like times, what to bring and price. Steep slope at 20–30 s means you're narrating what viewers can already see; cut words and let the visuals carry (target ~2.3 wps). High skip rate means "here's what happens" is generic; lead with a surprising detail ("no phones for 60 minutes").

### S5 — Transformation (FTC-safe version): "Turning a Tuesday 6 pm from chaos to calm" or "Studio before/after"

- **Goal:** Watch time plus sends.
- **Length:** 15–30 s.

| Beat | Seconds | Said | Shown |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook ("after" first) | 0–2 | none / VO: "This is 6:45 pm." | Text: "6:00 pm vs 6:45 pm". Calm lounge shot (the end state shown first). |
| Rewind | 2–5 | VO: "Here's 6:00." | Rewind effect to a chaotic commute, laptop, phone buzzing. |
| Process montage | 5–20 | VO (sparse): "Phone in the locker. Heat. Cold, if you want. Tea." | 6–8 cuts on a ~2–3 s rhythm, each a sensory detail (door, steam, splash, pour). |
| Return to "after" | 20–24 | VO: "Same Tuesday. Different evening." | Matches the opening frame, so it loops. |
| Send line | 24–27 | — | Text: "Send to your 6 pm-chaos coworker." |

- **Retention devices:** "after" first (open loop), rewind pattern-interrupt, montage rhythm, matched-frame loop.
- **Share/save lines:** "Send to your 6 pm-chaos coworker. Thursday slots are open." "Save for the next week that runs you over."
- **Evidence:** Before/after transformations are a top practitioner format (**PC**). For a wellness business, the "transformation" must be the *schedule or setting*, not a body, symptom, mood disorder or performance metric. Before/after body or health claims require RCT-level substantiation (**OS**, FTC 2022). "Calm" as a description of the *space* is a much safer framing than "reduces stress."
- **Failure signs → fix:** Low average watch time (under ~50% of length) means the montage lacks progression; order shots so each is clearly "next." Replays low means the end frame doesn't match the start; reshoot the end to match. Comments asking "does it help with X?" means don't answer with outcomes in comments either; reply "Here's what a session includes," then link the FAQ.

### S6 — Day-in-the-Life: "A day as NUMA's front-desk / session host"

- **Goal:** Follows plus connected-reach likes (community).
- **Length:** 30–60 s.

| Beat | Seconds | Said | Shown |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–3 | "6:30 am: I open NUMA. Here's the whole day." | Text: "Day in the life: NUMA host". Keys turning in the door. |
| Morning | 3–15 | Short VO lines per timestamp. | Timestamps on screen (6:30, 7:15, 9:00): heating the sauna, setting out towels, the first regulars. |
| Midday twist | 15–25 | "The part nobody sees: 40 minutes of cleaning between blocks." | Fast-paced clean montage. |
| Evening | 25–40 | "Then Numa Nights setup." | Lights, playlist, tea batch. |
| Close | 40–48 | "Follow for more of the behind-the-counter stuff." | Host waving with the lights dimming; text "Follow for the BTS." |

- **Retention devices:** timestamps (progress), an insider twist ("part nobody sees"), a recurring person (parasocial continuity).
- **Share/save lines:** "Send to the friend who'd be the best NUMA host." "Save for the next time you wonder what happens before you walk in."
- **Evidence:** Likes matter more for connected (follower) reach (**OS**). Day-in-the-life drives follows and community (**PC**). Sprout's 2025 Index puts authenticity and relatability at the top of what consumers want (**LS** survey). This is low-risk on FTC grounds; keep staff opinions about the *job and space*.
- **Failure signs → fix:** Good watch time but follows under ~1 per 1K reached means there's no series promise; brand it "Host Diaries, ep. 3" and pin a series highlight. Drop at the midday section means the cleaning montage runs too long; cap it at 6 s.

### S7 — Behind the Scenes: "How we reset the space between every session"

- **Goal:** Trust plus saves (quality signals for local service).
- **Length:** 15–35 s.

| Beat | Seconds | Said | Shown |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–2 | "You'll never see this part of NUMA." | Text: "What happens in the 15 min between sessions". Overhead time-lapse start. |
| Steps | 2–22 | Very sparse VO, or ASMR sound only. | 6–8 satisfying cuts (towels folded, water changed, benches wiped, tea refilled), each 2–3 s. |
| Payoff | 22–27 | "Ready for you." | Clean, empty, lit room, held for 2 s. |
| Save line | 27–30 | — | Text: "Save this for when you book: we reset every room, every time." |

- **Retention devices:** an "insider access" hook, oddly-satisfying process cuts, a clear end state, ASMR audio (works sound-on; captions cover sound-off).
- **Share/save lines:** "Send to the friend who's picky about clean spaces." "Save this: here's how every NUMA room is reset."
- **Evidence:** Process and BTS content is practitioner-favoured for trust (**PC**). Make hygiene claims only if they're literally true and documented. Avoid "sterile" or "germ-free" unless substantiated (**OS**, FTC general substantiation principle).
- **Failure signs → fix:** Skip rate high means the overhead opener is too slow; open mid-action (a towel snap). Saves low with good watch time means the content is watchable but not useful; add a "what you'll find in your room" checklist overlay at the end.

### S8 — Founder Story: "Why I started NUMA"

- **Goal:** Follows, profile visits, local affinity.
- **Length:** 45–90 s.

| Beat | Seconds | Said | Shown |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–3 | A specific, odd detail: "NUMA started with a broken kettle in my apartment." | Text: "Why NUMA exists (in 60 sec)". Founder at the tea bar, holding an old kettle. |
| Context | 3–15 | The relatable problem (no place in [city] to slow down with friends that wasn't a bar). | B-roll of the founder's past routine. Cut every 3–4 s. |
| Turning point | 15–35 | The moment of decision; one vulnerable line. | Empty-unit footage, first build days, photos. |
| What NUMA is now | 35–55 | Community, Numa Nights, the people (no outcome promises). | Faces of regulars (with consent), event lights. |
| Callback | 55–62 | "Still have the kettle." | Kettle on the shelf; it closes the loop on the hook. |
| CTA | 62–68 | "Come say hi at the next Numa Nights." | Text: date plus "link in bio." |

- **Retention devices:** a specific-object hook, a callback (open loop closed), vulnerability, faces.
- **Share/save lines:** "Send to someone who's always wanted to open their own place." "Send to your [city] friend who's tired of meeting at bars."
- **Evidence:** Absolute seconds watched matter, so a 60–90 s story is not penalized if retained (**OS**, Mosseri). Buffer finds 90–179 s Reels get slightly more reach against baseline (**LS**). Socialinsider shows engagement steady from 30 s to 3 min (**LS**). Founder content is mainly a follow and affinity play (**PC**).
- **Failure signs → fix:** Steep drop at 10–20 s means the backstory comes before stakes; move the turning point earlier and trim the context to 8 s. Average watch time under ~15 s on a 60 s Reel means you should cut to 40 s or split into a 2-part series. Profile visits but low follows means the bio needs a clear "what / where / book" line.

### S9 — Offer Promo: "This week at NUMA: intro pass"

- **Goal:** Link clicks, DMs, bookings (conversion, mostly from warm followers).
- **Length:** 12–25 s.

| Beat | Seconds | Said | Shown |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–2 | "If you've been meaning to try NUMA, this is the week." | Text: "First-timer pass: [price] this week". The real space, in motion. |
| What's included | 2–10 | "Sauna, plunge, tea lounge, 60 minutes, robe included." | Three quick cuts of each element. |
| Proof (non-health) | 10–14 | "Booked out the last two Thursdays." (only if true) | Booking calendar screen recording. |
| CTA | 14–20 | "Tap the link in bio, or DM us 'FIRST' and we'll send times." | Text: "Link in bio · DM 'FIRST'". |
| Terms | 20–22 | — | Text: "New guests only. Ends Sunday." |

- **Retention devices:** urgency (a real deadline), concrete price, a single CTA, short length.
- **Share/save lines:** "Send to whoever you'd split the intro pass with." "Save this. It ends Sunday."
- **Evidence:** Promotional and offer content gets lower organic distribution than value content, and contest or giveaway promotion limits recommendations (**OS**, creator FAQ). Meta's engagement-bait definition exempts "a specific call to action," so a keyword DM tied to receiving times is defensible, but how it's classified is unclear (**OS** plus thin). Any scarcity claim ("booked out") must be true (**OS**, FTC deception standard).
- **Failure signs → fix:** Reach mostly followers and low non-follower reach is expected; boost or pair with an S1/S2 Reel the same week. Link clicks low with good watch time means the CTA is buried; repeat it in text and the caption's first line, and use a DM keyword. Skip rate very high means it opens like an ad; open on a guest moment, not a price card.

- **Goal:** Sends ("bring a friend") plus RSVPs.
- **Length:** 15–30 s.

- **Share/save lines:** "Send to your Numa Nights +1." "Save the date: [day], [time], NUMA."
- **Evidence:** Sends dominate unconnected reach (**OS**). Metricool finds ~65% of a Reel's views land in the first 72 hours (**LS**), so post teasers 5–10 days out and again at 48 hours. Event teasers are a practitioner staple (**PC**).
- **Failure signs → fix:** Sends fine but RSVPs low means the CTA path is broken; put the RSVP in the bio's first link and a Story link sticker the same day. Low reach means the teaser is too similar to the last event's; change the first frame and hook line, since repetition drives skips (**PC**).

- **Length:** 20–40 s.

|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–2 | none | Best single moment first (laughter at the plunge, the DJ drop). Text: "You missed this 👀 (Numa Nights recap)". |

| Next date | 30–34 | — | Text: "Next Numa Nights: [date] · RSVP in bio". |

- **Retention devices:** best-moment-first, music-synced cuts (with captions so they read muted), an emotional arc, a "next date" open loop.
- **Share/save lines:** "Send to the friend who said they'd come next time." "Save for [next date]: Numa Nights, round [N]."
- **Evidence:** Recap formats are practitioner-standard (**PC**). Use original footage only; reposting already-posted Reels or watermarked clips reduces recommendation (**OS**). Get consent for identifiable guests, and quote guests only on the experience.
- **Failure signs → fix:** Big skip rate means the opener is a wide establishing shot; use the single most emotional close-up. Watch time drops mid-way means the montage has no arc; restructure into arrival, peak, wind-down. Low sends means there's no forward date; always end with the next event.

### S13 — Micro-Ritual Loop: "The 9-second NUMA moment"

- **Goal:** Watch time and replays (algorithmic reach), new-audience discovery.
- **Length:** 7–12 s.

| Beat | Seconds | Said | Shown |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook / whole thing | 0–3 | none, or ambient sound | Steam rolling off the water at the plunge edge. Text: "the sound of Thursday night at NUMA". |
| Action | 3–7 | ambient | A hand pours tea, or a robe sleeve is set down. |
| Seamless loop | 7–9 | ambient | Last frame equals the first frame. |

- **Retention devices:** a seamless loop (replays count toward views and watch time), ASMR, a text hook that invites a rewatch.
- **Share/save lines:** Caption: "Send to the friend you'd sit here with." Keep any send line in the caption; this Reel is too short for overlay CTAs.
- **Evidence:** Replays are included in views and watch time (**OS**, Instagram definitions). Socialinsider shows sub-30 s Reels have the lowest median views (4,700) and engagement (0.28%) (**LS**), so loops are a supporting format, not the backbone. Low risk for FTC.
- **Failure signs → fix:** Average watch time below the Reel's length (under ~100%) means the loop isn't seamless; match the first and last frames and the audio. Reach low means it's too ambient to hook; add movement and a curiosity text line in frame 1.

### S14 — Comparison "Which one is your vibe?": "Sauna vs. plunge vs. tea lounge person"

- **Goal:** Sends plus comments (identity-based).
- **Length:** 15–30 s.

| Beat | Seconds | Said | Shown |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–2 | "Which NUMA person are you?" | Split-screen of three zones. Text: "Sauna person / Plunge person / Tea-lounge person". |
| Profile 1 | 2–9 | Playful traits ("brings a book, stays 20 minutes, never talks"). | Sauna character vignette. |
| Profile 2 | 9–16 | ("counts down out loud, screams, does it again"). | Plunge vignette. |
| Profile 3 | 16–23 | ("here for the tea and the gossip"). | Lounge vignette. |
| Send line | 23–27 | "Send this to your opposite." | Text overlay. |

- **Retention devices:** identity self-sorting (viewers wait for "their" type), three-act repetition, humor.
- **Share/save lines:** "Send this to your opposite." "Tell us your type in the comments: we'll save you a spot by it at Numa Nights." (This is a genuine question, not "comment YES.")
- **Evidence:** Identity and personality content drives sends (**PC**). The engagement-bait line is crossed by unrelated asks ("comment YES"), while a genuine question is allowed (**OS**, Meta definition). Traits must be personality jokes, never physiological benefits ("plunge person recovers faster" is a claim).
- **Failure signs → fix:** Comments high but sends low means the types aren't relatable outside NUMA's regulars; make them universal (introvert, adrenaline, social). Drop between types means each vignette runs too long; cap at 6 s and add a beat change in each.

### S15 — Reply-to-Comment FAQ: "'Do I need to know what I'm doing?' Answering your question"

- **Goal:** Saves, comments, profile visits (objection handling).
- **Length:** 20–45 s.

| Beat | Seconds | Said | Shown |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–3 | Reads the question aloud. | Instagram comment-reply sticker on screen plus host face. |
| Answer | 3–25 | Three-part answer: what happens, who helps, what's optional. | Cut to the relevant zones; text keywords. |
| Boundary line | 25–30 | "We can't give medical advice. If you have health questions, ask your provider." | Host to camera; text repeats it. |
| Close | 30–35 | "Drop your next question. We'll answer it in a Reel." | Text: "Ask us anything about your first visit". |

- **Retention devices:** a real-viewer question (relevance), direct address, a series promise.
- **Share/save lines:** "Save this if you've been nervous about your first visit." "Send to the friend who asked you the same thing."
- **Evidence:** Instagram's creator FAQ recommends replying to comments. Buffer data scientist Julian Winternheimer's fixed-effects analysis of 700,000+ Instagram posts from ~68,000 accounts found that "posts where creators replied to comments saw about 21% higher engagement on average — even after controlling for whether the post had comments at all" (**OS** plus **LS**). Saying "we can't give medical advice" also protects against implied-claim drift (**OS**, FTC principles).
- **Failure signs → fix:** Skip rate high means the question isn't broadly relevant; choose the question asked most often. Saves low means the answer is vague; add numbers (times, prices, steps).

## Pacing, text and caption spec sheet (applies to all structures)

| Element | Spec for NUMA | Evidence level |
|---|---|---|
| Hook window | Visual change plus text hook by 0.5–1 s; promise stated by 3 s | OS (Instagram Creators says 3 s; business page says 5 s) + PC (Hootsuite: 1–2 s) |
| Speaking rate | 2.3–3.0 words per second; word budgets ≈ 15 s: 35–45 · 30 s: 70–85 · 60 s: 140–170 · 90 s: 200–250 | PC (NCVS 150 wpm baseline); AN for "winners 2.76–3.4 wps"; pace alone doesn't separate hits from flops (1,290-video study) |
| Visual change frequency | Every 2–4 s in talking-head Reels (cut, punch-in, B-roll, new text line); 1.5–2.5 s in music montages; hold climaxes 2–3 s uncut | PC; rigorous measurement absent (arXiv 2026 pace label only) |
| On-screen hook text | 5–8 words, in the top-middle safe zone, on screen 2–3 s | PC; "mostly text" Reels shown less (OS) |
| Captions | Burned-in or native captions on 100% of spoken Reels; high contrast; keep out of the bottom ~35% and top ~14% (ads safe-zone spec, a reasonable organic default) | LS survey (Verizon/Publicis 2019: 80% more likely to finish with captions); OS (safe zones for ads) |
| Audio | Original VO for explainers and FAQs; trending or ambient for loops, sketches and recaps; never post muted | OS (muted Reels shown less) + PC (Hootsuite) |
| Send/save line placement | Right after the payoff (last 15–25% of the Reel) plus the caption's first line; never in the first 3 s | PC |
| Loop | Final frame matches the opening frame on loops, sketches and transformations | OS (replays count) + PC |
| Disclosure | "Complimentary session" or "Paid partnership" on screen during the testimonial plus in the caption | OS (FTC Endorsement Guides / 2024 Rule) |

## Symptom in metrics → likely cause → fix to try

*Thresholds are directional. Instagram publishes no official benchmarks. Compare each Reel with NUMA's own median over its last 10–20 Reels and judge at 72 hours (Metricool: ~65% of views arrive by then).*

| # | Symptom in metrics | Likely cause | Fix to try next |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Skip rate well above your median (or roughly 50%+) | Opening frame is static, a logo or establishing shot; hook only works with sound | Open mid-action on a face or motion; add a 5–8 word text hook in frame 1; test two hooks via Trial Reels |
| 2 | Skip rate rising across consecutive Reels | Same first shot, same host angle or same hook template every time (recognition skips) | Rotate opening shot types (POV, close-up, overhead, text-on-black for a max of 1 s) |
| 3 | Skip rate low but retention cliff at 3–8 s | Hook promises something the next beat doesn't deliver; slow setup | Cut the setup; put the first payoff by 5–6 s; restate the promise in text |
| 4 | Retention curve has a sharp dip mid-Reel | A dead beat: long static shot, rambling line, or repeated information | Trim that segment; add a visual change every 2–4 s; move the strongest beat earlier |
| 5 | Average watch time under ~30–40% of length | Reel is longer than its idea; too many words per second or too much talk over visuals | Cut 20–30% of the runtime; drop to ~2.5 wps; let visuals carry the payoff |
| 6 | Average watch time over 100% but low reach | Good loop, but the hook doesn't stop cold viewers, or the topic is too niche | Keep the loop; strengthen the first-frame text; broaden the identity angle |
| 7 | Retention fine, reach stuck at followers (low non-follower share) | Low sends per reach; content addresses insiders only | Add a "send to [specific person]" payoff; use universal framing (S2, S14, S16); avoid NUMA jargon |
| 8 | Reach suddenly low on a specific Reel | Recommendation-ineligible: watermark, border, mostly text, muted, reposted footage, engagement-bait caption, giveaway promo | Re-export clean original footage; remove "tag/share/comment YES" language; keep giveaways off reach Reels |
| 9 | Sends per 1K reached low (well under ~5; wellness stretch ~10) | No clear recipient; the payoff is informative but not socially useful | Name the recipient ("your plunge buddy"); add a relatable POV or a shared plan (event date) |
| 10 | Saves per 1K reached low (well under ~3; wellness stretch ~7–8) | Content isn't a reference; no specifics worth returning to | Add times, prices, steps, a packing list; convert to a checklist (S12); cross-post as a carousel |
| 11 | High saves, low sends | Useful but private (people save for themselves) | Add a social hook: "send to whoever you're booking with"; tie it to an event date |
| 12 | High sends, low saves | Entertaining but not reusable | Pair it with a follow-up utility Reel or carousel; add one concrete takeaway at the end |
| 13 | Likes per reach low among followers | Content drifted toward cold-audience bait; followers want community and people | Post more S6/S8/S11 (faces, regulars, staff); reply to comments (Buffer, 700K+ posts: ~21% higher engagement) |
| 14 | Profile visits high, follows low | Bio unclear (what, where, how to book); grid doesn't show a series | Rewrite the bio: "Recovery-space & social wellness club · [Neighborhood] · Book ↓"; pin 3 Reels (S4, S3, S10) |

## Recommendations

1. **Weekly mix (3–5 Reels/week).** Buffer's 2.1M-post frequency study found 3–5 posts/week roughly doubled follower growth versus 1–2. Each week, post two send-builders (S1, S2, S14 or S16), one save-builder (S4, S12 or S15), one people Reel (S3, S6 or S8), and one event or offer Reel (S9–S11) in event weeks.
2. **Run every new hook as a Trial Reel first.** Compare Trial Reels only with each other (Mosseri). Promote the hook with the lowest skip rate to a full post.
3. **Score each Reel on four numbers at 72 hours:** skip rate, average watch time ÷ length, sends per 1K reach, saves per 1K reach. Track them against NUMA's rolling median. Use Dash Social's derived wellness averages (~10.5 sends and ~7.6 saves per 1K) as stretch references only.
4. **Adopt a claims checklist before posting.** No outcome verbs; disclose comps; real guests only; add the provider line wherever heat or cold appears; record consent for identifiable guests. Have counsel approve a standing "approved phrases" list.
5. **Build event cycles around Numa Nights.** Post S10 at T−7 to −10 days, S2 or S14 at T−3, S10 short-cut at T−2, S11 at T+1 with the next date, then S3 from a first-time attendee.

## Caveats

- **Length studies conflict.** Socialinsider favors 45–60 s for likes and comments per follower; Buffer favors 90–179 s for reach against baseline. Neither covers wellness or local service, and Socialinsider's engagement metric excludes sends and saves. Test with your own data.
- **Sends and saves benchmarks per reach are not published by any major study.** The ~10.5 and ~7.6 per 1K wellness figures are derived from Dash Social averages of mostly large brands, and small local accounts may differ widely.
- **Pacing evidence is the thinnest area.** No rigorous study ties cuts per second or words per second to Reels retention. The specs above are practitioner consensus, and widely shared numeric claims ("1.5–2 s shots = +22% retention") are unsourced.
- **Sound-off figures conflict** (from ~50% to 85%+, with some claims of 80% sound-on). The 2019 Verizon/Publicis data predates Reels' maturity. Captioning is cheap insurance either way.
- **Skip-rate "good" thresholds are unofficial.** The April 2026 Insights update that surfaced share rate and skip rate percentages is documented in Social Media Today's April 26, 2026 report of Instagram's announcement, not on an Instagram primary page, and it does not cover collab posts, trial reels, cross-posted or boosted content.
- FTC rules apply to US advertising. This report is not legal advice, and NUMA should have recurring claim language reviewed.
