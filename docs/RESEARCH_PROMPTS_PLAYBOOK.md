# Deep research prompts: NUMA Studio playbook seed

Run each prompt below as a separate **Research** request in the Claude app. Save each result as a Google Doc (or .md) in Drive under `NUMA Reels/_Plan/Research/`, named `R1 Hooks`, `R2 Formats and mix`, and so on. NUMA Studio imports them into its playbook. The output format at the end of each prompt is what makes the import work, so leave it in.

Every prompt shares this context (it is already included in each one):

> NUMA Wellness Collective is a contrast-therapy and breathwork studio in Midtown Mobile, Alabama (Finnish sauna at 175-190°F, 4 cold plunges at 44-52°F, red light, breathwork, yoga, Flow Space, plus counseling, massage and facials under one roof; $49 "Discover Numa" one-month trial that ends on its own; special events such as Numa Nights about every 4 months). Always write the name "Numa", never "NUMA". Instagram @numawellnessmobile, a small local business account (under 5,000 followers). Competitors nearby are tiny (largest ~3,000 followers). The account posts reels, carousels, single photos and Stories, and runs Trial Reels. All claims must follow FTC rules: sell the experience, never health outcomes.

## R1: Hooks and openings that stop the scroll

```
Context: NUMA Wellness Collective is a contrast-therapy and breathwork studio in Midtown Mobile, Alabama (Finnish sauna at 175-190°F, 4 cold plunges at 44-52°F, red light, breathwork, yoga, Flow Space, plus counseling, massage and facials under one roof; $49 "Discover Numa" one-month trial that ends on its own; special events such as Numa Nights about every 4 months). Always write the name "Numa", never "NUMA". Instagram @numawellnessmobile, a small local business account (under 5,000 followers). Competitors nearby are tiny. The account posts reels, carousels, single photos and Stories, and runs Trial Reels. All claims must follow FTC rules: sell the experience, never health outcomes.

Research what makes the first 1-3 seconds of an Instagram Reel (and the first slide of a carousel) stop the scroll for wellness, sauna, cold plunge, breathwork and small local studio accounts in 2025-2026. Use measured studies (Socialinsider, Buffer, Metricool, Later, Hootsuite, Meta/Instagram official statements) and specific account examples with numbers (Bathhouse, SweatHouz, Othership, Remedy Place, local studios). Separate what is measured from what is observed or opinion.

Deliver a catalogue of 25-40 hook patterns. For EACH pattern give exactly these fields:
- id (short kebab-case), name
- format(s) it applies to: reel | carousel | photo | story
- template (fill-in-the-blank wording) and 2 NUMA-specific example lines that are FTC-safe
- visual for frame 0 (what must be on screen)
- best use (series/goal: education, community, offer, event, local/seasonal, collab)
- evidence: measured | observed | opinion, with source links and any numbers
- expected effect on: skip rate, average watch time, sends, saves
- when NOT to use it
- what to try instead if it underperforms
End with the 10 patterns you would test first for this account and why.
```

## R2: Post type mix, cadence and timing

```

Use measured studies first (Buffer 2.1M posts, Socialinsider, Metricool, Later, Hootsuite, Sprout), Instagram/Meta official guidance (Mosseri), then account examples.

Deliver:
1. A table of post types with: best goal, typical reach vs engagement vs saves vs sends behaviour, recommended share of feed posts, evidence level + sources.
2. Cadence rules (posts per week, max per day, spacing, what happens after a dark week) with evidence.
3. Timing: what is known about best days/hours for local service businesses in US Central time, how much it matters vs content quality, and how to find an account's own best times (method + minimum data needed).
4. Monthly rhythm: how to place offers/discounts, events (teaser/recap), education, community and seasonal posts across a month (e.g. offers near paydays/month start, New Year/heat-wave timing), with evidence.
5. A starting default schedule for NUMA (week by week for one month) marked as a hypothesis, and the 5 most valuable experiments to run in the first 60 days to learn NUMA's own optimum.

Deliver 12-20 structures. For EACH: id, name, goal, length range, beat-by-beat outline (beat, seconds, what is said, what is shown), retention devices used, share/save line examples (FTC-safe, NUMA-specific), evidence level + sources + numbers, failure signs in metrics (e.g. drop at 3 s, low sends) and the fix to try next. Also deliver a table "symptom in metrics -> likely cause -> fix to try" with at least 15 rows (skip rate, average watch time, retention %, reach, sends/1k, saves/1k, profile visits, follows, link clicks).
```

## R6: Learning loop: how to measure and adapt

```
Context: [same NUMA context as above]

Research practical methods for a small Instagram account (20-25 posts a month) to learn what works from its own engagement data without being fooled by noise: which metrics to optimise for each goal (reach, sends, saves, follows, profile visits, link clicks, bookings), normalising by reach and by the account's own medians, how long to wait before judging a post (Instagram data lag, ~72 hours for most views), minimum sample sizes, explore vs exploit (e.g. Thompson sampling / multi-armed bandits for post type, time slot, hook pattern), using Trial Reels as A/B tests, seasonality and holiday effects, and when to retire or promote a pattern.

Deliver: (1) a recommended scoring formula per goal with weights and why; (2) decision rules with thresholds (e.g. "promote a hook pattern after N tries if its median score beats the account median by X"); (3) an explore/exploit policy suited to ~20 posts a month (how many slots to spend on experiments, which variables to test first); (4) pitfalls and how to avoid them (novelty effects, follower growth changing baselines, viral outliers); (5) sources for each recommendation, with evidence level.
```

## R7: How long to wait before judging a post

Save the result as `R7 Judging windows`. NUMA Studio imports it into the playbook's judging-window policy, which replaces the spec default of an early read at 72 h and a final score at 7 days.

```
Context: [same NUMA context as above]

Research how long a small Instagram business account (under 5,000 followers, 20-25 posts a month) should wait after posting before judging how a post performed, for each post type: Reels, Trial Reels, carousels, single-image posts, and Stories. Use, in this order: Meta/Instagram official documentation (Graph API insights data delay, metric availability windows, Stories' 24-hour insights limit, Trial Reels test period), statements from Instagram leadership (Mosseri), measured studies of view and engagement accumulation over time (Socialinsider, Buffer, Metricool, Later, Hootsuite, Emplifi, Dash Hudson, academic papers), then practitioner reports. Separate what is measured from what is observed or opinion, and say when a source is about large accounts only.

Answer:
1. Data lag: how long Instagram insights take to become complete and stable for each metric (reach, views, plays, average watch time, skip rate, likes, comments, saves, shares/sends, profile visits, follows), with sources.
2. Accumulation curves: what share of a post's eventual reach and engagement typically arrives by 24 h, 48 h, 72 h, 7 days, 14 days and 30 days, per post type and for small accounts. Do saves and sends accumulate more slowly than likes? How does a long tail (Reels resurfacing, Explore, search, sends) change this?
3. Early indicators: which early metrics (for example the first 24-72 h skip rate, average watch time, or sends per reach) predict the 7-day or 30-day result well enough to act on early, with correlation or accuracy figures if they exist.
4. Trial Reels: when its results are final, and what to compare against.
5. Stories: how to capture data before the 24-hour limit, and when to judge.
6. Pitfalls: posts that start slowly and grow, posts boosted by a collab or a share, weekends and holidays, and how to avoid judging too early or too late.

Deliver a table with ONE row per post type (reel, trial_reel, carousel, image, story) and exactly these columns:
- post_type
- ignore_before_hours (no judgment before this)
- provisional_hours (early read)
- provisional_weight (0-1, how much an early read should count)
- final_hours (when the score is final)
- best_early_metric (the metric to read first, and why)
- evidence: measured | observed | opinion
- confidence: high | medium | low
- sources (links)
- how to verify on NUMA's own data (for example: compare 72 h against 7 d scores after 20 posts and adjust the weight)
Then list any metric-specific exceptions (for example "saves: judge at 7 d, not 72 h") in the same column format, with a metric column added.
```
